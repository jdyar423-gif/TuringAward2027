## Related work

**Training to a deadline.** The nearest settings are Cramming, a BERT-style masked language model trained from scratch on one consumer GPU in one day [1], and the 24-hour recipe of Izsak et al. on one 8-GPU server [2]; both tie the learning-rate decay to the time budget, as budgeted training recommends [3]. Speedruns fix a target and minimise time on an 8 × H100 node: a FineWeb loss of 3.28 in modded-nanogpt [4], GPT-2's CORE score in nanochat, whose script runs the whole chat pipeline [5]. Our schedule is of this kind: progress p = T/(T + R), with T the seconds trained and R the seconds left before the deadline, sets the learning rate, the sequence length and the data mixture; only its survival of a reclaimed runtime and of a change of configuration is particular. What differs from all four is the product: a chat model trained on one accelerator chip and compared blind with released models.

**Small open models.** MobileLLM found deep-and-thin shapes and shared embeddings to matter below 10⁹ parameters, and computing each block twice to add 0.7–0.8 points of average accuracy [6]; MobileLLM-R1 pre-trains on 4.2 T tokens resampled from about 2 T curated ones [7], and R1.5 adds on-policy distillation [8] from a 4B teacher [9]. Falcon-H1 runs attention and state-space heads in parallel [10]; its 90M instruct model saw 800 G tokens, with instruction data a quarter of the mixture from the start, and one light DPO epoch raised IFEval from 50.1 to 66.1 while more epochs degraded it [11]. SmolLM2 trains 135M and 360M models on 2 T and 4 T tokens [12], and TinyStories showed fluency below 10⁷ parameters in a narrow domain [13]. Our run will have seen on the order of 10¹⁰ tokens (7.79 × 10⁹ at the graft; final count [TBD: at export, 24 Nov 2026]). It uses no logit or on-policy distillation, but much of its chat data was written by larger models (SmolTalk's MagPie-Ultra, for instance, by Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct [12]).

**Weight-shared depth.** Re-applying one block goes back to the Universal Transformer [14] and ALBERT [15]. A k-layer model looped L times nearly matches a kL-layer one on synthetic reasoning [16], and recurrent depth has been scaled to 3.5 × 10⁹ parameters [17] and 7.7 T tokens [18]. Two 2026 studies measure what looping buys. Parcae stabilises it by constraining the spectral norm of the injection [19]; Schwethelm et al. fit L = E + A(N_once + r^φ N_rec)^−α + B D^−β with φ = 0.46, so that at equal training compute shared recurrences lose to unique layers, while two-lane hyper-connections [20] raise φ to 0.65 [21]. Our core (2 shared blocks × 8 loops, two lanes, MuonH) is ported from the code of [21]. Our reason to loop differs: under a read budget a re-read weight counts once, so looping buys compute per number read, not per FLOP; and dropping their prelude and coda (N_once = 0) applies the law outside its fitted range. The per-loop value embeddings are the value embeddings of the speedrun line [4, 5], after value residual learning [22], indexed by loop instead of layer.

**Vocabulary-indexed parameters and two counts.** Capacity that is stored but not read for every token takes many forms: feed-forward weights chosen by hashing the current token [23], trainable key-value memory layers [24, 25], larger input vocabularies, whose loss falls by 0.0256 per decade of added rows for OLMoE-1.3B models at 500 G tokens [26], n-gram embeddings kept off the accelerator [27, 28], and L3, which aggregates per-token keys and values by attention from the hidden state and gives frequent tokens more rows [29]. v9's token neurons are closest to L3: each vocabulary id owns 8 keys and values per shared block in a common 192-dimensional space, read through ReLU² instead of a softmax, allocated uniformly and re-read in all 8 loops. On which side of attention such parameters help, SwitchHead's routed experts on values and outputs reach 12.27 perplexity against 12.31 dense and 12.90 on queries and keys [30], and Memory Attention, with keys shared with values, found token memory best on values and worst on queries, keys and attention outputs (final loss 2.553 against 2.612–2.650) [31]: a caution for our multiplicative output gains, measured only in a small laboratory (d = 48, 1.2 × 10⁷ tokens, one seed). Counting the numbers read to produce one token from the cached state, our standard reading, is the mixture-of-experts convention (Mixtral: 4.7 × 10¹⁰ parameters available, 1.3 × 10¹⁰ active [32]); Gemma 3n is named by its about 1.9 × 10⁹ "effective" of more than 5 × 10⁹ loaded parameters [33]. v9 meets only the standard reading; the strict one, the union over the context window, is 23.8 M (median of 40 documents at 1,024 positions). We found no published counterpart of our read-set audit, a reference forward pass that receives only the rows read.

**Copying and exact counts.** Mixing the network's distribution with a copy distribution goes back to pointer-sentinel models [34] and the continuous cache, which linked it to count-based caches [35]; kNN-LM [36] and infini-gram [37] interpolate with neighbours and with unbounded n-gram counts. Our output law, p(v) = (q(v) + Σ_k m_k[c_k = v]) / (1 + Σ_k m_k) with masses learned from host-computed 1–3-gram count features, contains the Pitman–Yor predictive [38] with the network as base measure as a special case: a known rule, not a new model. Attention learns similar statistics through induction heads [39], and hard-wired n-gram heads lowered the perplexity of 340M-parameter models by 6.7% [40]. Repetitions self-reinforce in neural language models [41]; in our gate on a 10⁶-token micro model, tempering the whole mixture sent 27% of replies into loops at temperature 0.5, against none with temperature on the network's part only and self-generated evidence weighted ×0.25. The per-head sink with a learned value follows the sinks of StreamingLLM and gpt-oss [42, 43] and was neutral in our ablation (−0.07%). The written library of our first configuration counted its corpus against the size budget, as EfficientQA did [44]; v9 has none.

**Changing a model during training.** Net2Net [45] and network morphism [46] grow networks by function-preserving transformations. Staged training applies growth operators to the whole training state, copying Adam moments for existing layers and zeroing them for new ones, and asks that loss and training dynamics both be preserved [47]; LLaMA Pro adds blocks that zero-initialised output projections make identities [48], sparse upcycling turns a dense checkpoint into experts [49], and LoRA starts its new path at zero [50]. Our graft is a loss-preserving operator of this family: 59 shared leaves (34,531,720 numbers) keep values, optimiser slots and update count, the time ledger continues p from 0.505, and 5 new leaves (63,504,384 numbers) start at an initialisation that adds exactly zero (loss difference 0.0 on a synthetic batch at steps 26,890, 28,501 and 29,722). What differs is the growth, vocabulary-indexed tables and a feed-forward branch holding 65% of the final stored numbers, made at half time because v9 trained from scratch would have seen fewer tokens; the training-dynamics property of [47] was not tested.

**Optimisers.** Muon orthogonalises the momentum of hidden matrices [51]; weight decay and update rescaling make it scale [52]; Polar Express supplies our iteration [53]; NorMuon [54] and cautious weight decay [55] refine it. MuonH, which we use as [21] does, keeps each matrix at a fixed Frobenius norm [56] and transfers learning rates with HyperP's data exponent 0.32 [57]; Muon with NorMuon and cautious decay did not differ from it in our micro ablation (−0.24%, margin 0.84%).

**Kernels and memory-bound execution.** FlashAttention made attention IO-aware [58] and its successors raised GPU utilisation [59]; on TPUs such kernels are written in Pallas [60], like JAX's splash attention [61] and the tokamax library [62]. Ivanov et al. found transformer training on GPUs memory-bound and cut data movement by up to 22.91% [63]. Our measurements on one v6e chip agree: 62% of a block application was layout glue, and removing it, together with larger attention tiles, made one forward and backward pass 1.79 times faster; a one-tile kernel that holds a (row, head) slice in on-chip memory and keeps its output through rematerialisation, with a hand-written ReLU² backward, raised the live rate from 239.5 k to 281–283 k tokens/s. These apply known principles; they are not new algorithms.

**Particles and constraints at decoding.** Sequential Monte Carlo steers language models with probabilistic programs [64], learned twists [65] and syntactic and semantic potentials that let small models outperform models over 8 times larger [66], and serves inference-time scaling [67]; masking without reweighting samples a distorted distribution [68]. The bound log K − (K−1)/K on the divergence of best-of-K selection from the base model is known, and only an upper bound [69]; majority votes are self-consistency [70]; our constraint families follow IFEval [71], forced calculator results GSM8K's annotations [72], and the budget forcing applied to MobileLLM-R1.5 is from s1 [73]. Our stand-in measurement on Falcon-H1-Tiny (all constraints met in 66%, 91% and 98% of prompts) tests an implementation, not a method.

**Preference optimisation and judging.** DPO [74] is a standard last stage: with UltraFeedback in Zephyr (7B) [75, 76], at 135M in SmolLM2 [12] and 90M in Falcon-H1-Tiny [11], and between SFT and reinforcement learning in Tulu 3 [77]; our single epoch at learning rates 1e-6 to 3e-6 follows Falcon-H1-Tiny's choice [11]. Our mixture includes the Tulu 3 SFT data, whose CoCoNot, WildJailbreak and WildGuardMix subsets teach refusals [77]; our loader drops conversations whose assistant turns open with stock refusals or deflections, so our model is not trained to refuse. Language-model judges favour one position, longer replies and their own style [78, 79, 80, 81]; we count a win only when both orders agree, the conservative rule of [78], and report outcomes by length ratio [80]; which judges decide the final comparison, and whether a second family checks it [81], is [TBD: fixed before the test split is opened].

### Positioning

The setting, a chat model trained from scratch within 24 hours of one TPU v6e chip and within 10⁸ stored and 2 × 10⁷ read numbers per token, judged blind against released models trained on 8 × 10¹¹ and more than 4.2 × 10¹² tokens, is the chat-model counterpart of Cramming [1] and must be compared with nanochat [5], which trains a chat model end to end on an 8 × H100 node. Spending the stored-but-unread budget on vocabulary-indexed pages on the writing side of a looped dense core is new as a combination; its parts must be compared with L3 for token-owned keys and values [29], with SwitchHead and Memory Attention for where token-keyed parameters help [30, 31], and with Schwethelm et al. for the core [21]. Switching a running training to a larger configuration by a function-preserving graft of the whole training state, at p = 0.505 and adding 65% of the final stored numbers, is an instance of the growth operators of staged training [47], distinguished only by the kind of growth and the wall-clock setting. Counting numbers read per token and auditing that count with a reference forward pass that sees only the rows read extends the active-parameter convention of mixture-of-experts models [32] and Gemma 3n [33]; we found no precedent for the audit, and v9 fails the stricter reading the audit also reports.
