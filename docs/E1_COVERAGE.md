# E1 — library coverage (written library v2)

Generated by `scripts/measure_coverage.py` (stages `pool`, `sweep`, `lib`, `report`) from the saved library `data_cache/library/lib_v2`. Every number is measured; nothing is extrapolated. Hits: a wiki passage is a hit when a normalised answer string occurs as a whole-token sequence in its text (title line + body); a qa entry when an answer string occurs in its stored answer (qa EM: stored answer equals an answer after SQuAD normalisation); a head-entity probe when the passage belongs to the probed article. These are proxies: a passage can contain the answer string by chance, and can answer a question without containing it.

Evaluation questions: NQ-open validation 3608 (all with answers), TriviaQA rc.nocontext validation 3000 (seeded sample of the 9,961 unique questions), PopQA 3000 (seeded sample), head-entity probes 2000 and common-name probes 2000 (evaluation half of the titles), Dolly-15k 1600 instructions and the 200 dev-half proxy prompts (first turns). Dev data (every choice: index size, gate): 3000 stored NQ-open train and 3000 stored TriviaQA train questions asked with their own qa entry excluded (leave-one-out), 2000 head-entity and 2000 common-name probes for the dev half of the titles, 2000 contrastive questions (perturbed stored questions of the dev half of the store) and 139 conversation turns that must retrieve nothing.

**Fair measurements.** NQ-open and TriviaQA have a known overlap between their train and test questions (paraphrases of the same question), and the qa layer stores the train questions, so their qa numbers are reported separately for test questions with and without a near-duplicate stored question. PopQA (templated questions about long-tail entities, no train split stored) and the proxy prompts are the fair out-of-distribution measurements.

## Review fixes: before / after

A review of this library showed that a wrong entry placed before a question produces a confident wrong answer: stored answers of a different question (another year, ordinal, polarity or entity), a person's common name answered with a film or a unit, follow-up and chat messages answered with unrelated articles through the exact-title rule, stale answers. What changed (`bes/library`):

- **Contrast check** (`qa.contrast_mismatch`): a stored question is dropped as a candidate when it differs from the user's in its numbers (digit tokens, ordinals, roman numerals, compared as numbers), order words (last, next, ...), degree words (most, least, superlatives, comparatives), relation words (before, after, since, until) or number of negations, or when a capitalised name of either side is missing from the other. Up to three candidates are read. Decided by measurement (old library and gate, top candidates returned at p >= 0.328 on dev NQ, dev TriviaQA, NQ-open val and TriviaQA val): the candidates each class removes were 22-70% correct (relation words 50-83% on groups of 8 to 23 questions), below the 0.8 target, so a veto, not a gate feature (details in the notes).
- **Primary topics** (`titles.TitleTable`): a plain title owns its key; a variant (disambiguated, 'The X', plural, stopword-dropped) or a person article (surname or the single name its article uses) owns a key only when it leads every other article containing it by 1.0 in importance; who-questions look among persons (lead by 1.0, importance without the bare-name mentions a disambiguated title shares with its namesakes); ambiguous keys are not stored, so they give no exact-title credit; a title word the article never writes in lower case is not plural-stemmed, nor is a capitalised query word. Exact-title credit only for identity requests (who/what is X, tell me about X, define X, a bare name); a one-term key needs such a frame or a capitalised name.
- **Conversation turns** (`retrieve.turn_type`, `Library.retrieve(..., context=...)`): a later turn that leans on an earlier one (it, its, he, she, they, that, 'the second one', 'what about X?', fewer than two content terms without a capitalised name) retrieves nothing; chat and acknowledgements retrieve nothing in any turn.
- **Time filter**: rules for most followed / viewed / subscribed, 'got the most', counts of seasons, episodes, stores, members ... 'are there' / 'does X have', 'still have', the oldest / richest / tallest living, office holders without 'current' ('who is france's president', 'who runs X').
- **Gate**: refitted on dev data with the contrastive questions, common-name probes and conversation negatives; a negative set's rows weigh like rows of the largest dev set; operating point: the smallest threshold at which every dev set with right answers reaches precision 0.8 (was 0.5) and the negative sets return a known-wrong entry for at most 5% of their queries.
- **Provenance** (pool index, title importance) is written next to the library (`data_cache/library/provenance/<hash of library.json>/`), no longer inside the shipped directory.

## Gate model

Two logistic regressions fitted on the dev rows (each dev set with right answers weighted equally, a row of the contrastive or conversation sets like a row of the largest dev set; L2 1e-3), float32, 50 stored numbers with the threshold. The threshold is the smallest at which every dev set with right answers separately reaches precision >= 0.8 and the contrastive and conversation sets return a known-wrong entry for at most 5% of their queries.

## Notes

- Token counts: passages are cut at 160 tokens of the model's own BPE tokenizer (title line + body). The v1 pool used UTF-8 bytes / 3.3; on Simple English Wikipedia the BPE averages about 3.7 bytes per token, so the cap moved from about 528 to about 590 bytes. Most articles are one-passage stubs, which is why the passage count changed by only 0.9%. Counting a candidate passage line by line (count(title + newline) + count(line + newline) ... + count(last line)) equals counting the whole text, because a newline followed by a non-space character always ends a BPE pre-token; this was verified on 20,000 pool passages and is checked again on every finished passage (fallback to whole-text counting).
- Non-Latin headings: a section heading in Devanagari or similar scripts costs about 3 tokens per character; a heading that takes more than half of a passage on its own is now left out (it made 1,480 passages exceed the cap in an intermediate build; the rendered text was always capped at retrieval time).
- Head-entity look-ups: BM25 alone answers 'Who was Napoleon?' with 'Napoleon Dynamite' and 'What is DNA?' with 'DNA polymerase' (short passages win the length normalisation). The library therefore stores an exact title look-up (`bes.library.titles`; hashes of the content terms of titles and of person names, 4 numbers per key). A plain title owns its key (Wikipedia's primary topic); a variant title (disambiguated, 'The X', plural, stopword-dropped) or a person article (by surname or the single name its article uses) owns a key only when it leads every other article containing the key by 1.0 in importance (about 2.7 times the mentions); keys without such an owner are not stored. The stored importance credits each disambiguated title with an equal share of every mention of its bare name (`select.mention_counts` splits a shared key: 'Lincoln (movie)' 9.02 against 'Abraham Lincoln' 8.18, 'Newton (unit)' 8.19 against 'Isaac Newton' 7.22), so a variant rarely clears the margin against a namesake person; who-questions rank persons without that share. Before the review a key went to the title without a disambiguator, else the more important title, and an ambiguous key still gave exact-title credit.
- Question-answer layer: the time-sensitive filter is deliberately aggressive (precision over coverage); it also removes some stable questions (for example 'Who is the only US President never to have married?' and questions that mention 'now' in a stable sense). Normalised duplicates whose answers disagree are dropped together. TriviaQA rc.nocontext has one row per evidence document (138,384 train rows = 76,523 unique questions); the store keeps each question once. The review added rules for audience records (most followed / viewed / subscribed, 'got the most'), current counts ('how many seasons of X are there', 'how many stores does X have'), 'still have', living superlatives (oldest / richest / tallest person, tallest building) and office holders without 'current' ('who is france's president', 'who runs X'); 1,811 more questions are dropped, some of them stable.
- Dev labels are strict: an NQ-open train question carries usually one annotated answer span, so a stored paraphrase with another correct span counts as a miss; dev qa precision is therefore a lower bound. The NQ-open validation questions carry up to five annotations.
- Gate operating point: the threshold is the smallest at which every dev set with right answers separately (NQ train LOO, TriviaQA train LOO, head-entity probes, common-name probes) reaches precision 0.8 (0.5 before the review: wrong passages cost more than missing ones) and the contrastive and conversation dev sets return a known-wrong entry for at most 5% of their queries. A pooled floor with equal set weights chose p >= 0.06 in an intermediate build of the first version: 91% pooled dev coverage at precision 0.52, but dev NQ alone 82% coverage at 27% precision, hidden by the easy probe set. In the first refit with the new dev sets, every dev set weighed the same: the 16 contrastive rows that pass the contrast check outweighed 1,825 NQ rows, the weight of sym became negative and the threshold 0.966 (no NQ or TriviaQA coverage); a row of a negative set now weighs like a row of the largest dev set.
- The index sweep ran before the primary-topic rules of the title table (both versions); the differences between its rows come from the index alone. It used dev questions only; the evaluation sets were measured once, on the final library.
- The cross-check feature (the qa answer occurs in the wiki candidate's text) reads the wiki candidate's text block for every query that has a qa candidate; this is included in the read set above.
- Head-entity probes are synthetic ('What is X?', 'Who was X?', 'Tell me about X.', 'X', ...) for the 10,000 most important titles of each half of a hash split of the titles; their label is 'the returned passage belongs to the probed article'. Since the review a disambiguated title is asked with its disambiguator ('What is Olympia (Manet)?': its bare name may mean another article, so a bare-name probe was labelled by construction) and person articles get who-templates, the others what-templates (before, 25.6% of the dev probes asked a bare name with a disambiguated label). Common-name probes ask 'Who was <name>?' for the most important person articles of each half, by the name their article uses most, when no more important person article bears it; label: the person's article.
- v1 comparison: the v1 E1 drew its TriviaQA dev and evaluation samples from the 17,944 validation rows, which hold only 9,961 unique questions (one row per evidence document), so its dev and evaluation samples could share questions; v2 samples unique questions and takes its TriviaQA dev questions from the train split.
- The v1 pool's passages reached 510 BPE tokens (non-Latin text under the bytes / 3.3 proxy); every v2 passage is at most 160 tokens (checked over the whole pool).
- Query-type filter: an earlier version treated any first/second-person word as a personal message, which excluded 7% of NQ-open, 5% of TriviaQA and 3% of PopQA questions (song titles such as 'What genre is Thinking of You?', regnal numerals such as 'David I of Scotland', quoted phrases). The final rule ignores quoted text and everything after a title word and counts only the asker or the assistant as subject; search-style phrases of up to twelve words are look-ups, and word problems (two numbers and an arithmetic cue) are math. The gate was refitted after this change. Greetings, wishes, thanks and acknowledgements ('merry christmas', 'thanks, that was helpful', 'try again') are chat ('other') since the review.
- Conversation turns: `Library.retrieve(query, context=earlier user turns)` returns nothing for a later turn that leans on an earlier one (`retrieve.turn_type`: it / its / he / she / they / that / 'the second one' without a name or three content terms of its own, 'what about X?', 'and in France?', fewer than two content terms without a capitalised name). The responder (`bes/decode/respond.py`) and the training-side retrieval for later turns (`bes/data/rag_format.py`) pass the message alone when this was measured; the first-turn rules (identity frames, chat) apply without the context.
- Contrast check (`qa.contrast_mismatch`), measured before choosing a veto over a gate feature (old library and gate, top qa candidates returned at p >= 0.328, dev NQ / dev TriviaQA / NQ-open val / TriviaQA val): those whose stored question differs in digit tokens were 40-62% correct (n = 68 to 101), in ordinal words 22-59% (n = 32 to 52), in degree words 30-70% (n = 17 to 30), in polarity 33-50% (n = 4 to 9), in relation words 50-83% (n = 8 to 23) and in capitalised names 56-67% (n = 168 to 191): all but one small group below the 0.8 target. Names need case information: a lower-case question that differs from a stored lower-case question in one place name is not caught (the extra row of the perturbation table); a veto on rare substituted terms would have removed dev returns that are 75-83% correct at the operating point.
- Provenance (pool index of every passage, title and name importance) is written to `data_cache/library/provenance/<first 16 hex digits of the SHA-256 of library.json>/`, not inside the library: an export copies the library directory whole, and the old `provenance/` folder (3,080,684 16-bit numbers as int64/float64 arrays) was shipped without being counted.
