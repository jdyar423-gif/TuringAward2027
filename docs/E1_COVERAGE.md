# E1 — library coverage (written library v2)

Generated by `scripts/measure_coverage.py` (stages `pool`, `sweep`, `lib`, `report`) from the saved library `data_cache/library/lib_v2`. Every number is measured; nothing is extrapolated. Hits: a wiki passage is a hit when a normalised answer string occurs as a whole-token sequence in its text (title line + body); a qa entry when an answer string occurs in its stored answer (qa EM: stored answer equals an answer after SQuAD normalisation); a head-entity probe when the passage belongs to the probed article. These are proxies: a passage can contain the answer string by chance, and can answer a question without containing it.

Evaluation questions: NQ-open validation 3608 (all with answers), TriviaQA rc.nocontext validation 3000 (seeded sample of the 9,961 unique questions), PopQA 3000 (seeded sample), head-entity probes 2000 (evaluation half of the titles), Dolly-15k 1600 instructions and the 200 dev-half proxy prompts (first turns). Dev data (every choice: index size, gate): 3000 stored NQ-open train and 3000 stored TriviaQA train questions asked with their own qa entry excluded (leave-one-out), and 2000 probes for the dev half of the titles.

**Fair measurements.** NQ-open and TriviaQA have a known overlap between their train and test questions (paraphrases of the same question), and the qa layer stores the train questions, so their qa numbers are reported separately for test questions with and without a near-duplicate stored question. PopQA (templated questions about long-tail entities, no train split stored) and the proxy prompts are the fair out-of-distribution measurements.

R@1 = hit of the single candidate without the gate and without the query-type filter (qa: 0 when no stored question reaches the minimum overlap); coverage = share of questions for which an entry is returned; precision = share of returned entries that are hits; answered = coverage x precision. ## Notes

- Token counts: passages are cut at 160 tokens of the model's own BPE tokenizer (title line + body). The v1 pool used UTF-8 bytes / 3.3; on Simple English Wikipedia the BPE averages about 3.7 bytes per token, so the cap moved from about 528 to about 590 bytes. Most articles are one-passage stubs, which is why the passage count changed by only 0.9%. Counting a candidate passage line by line (count(title + newline) + count(line + newline) ... + count(last line)) equals counting the whole text, because a newline followed by a non-space character always ends a BPE pre-token; this was verified on 20,000 pool passages and is checked again on every finished passage (fallback to whole-text counting).
- Non-Latin headings: a section heading in Devanagari or similar scripts costs about 3 tokens per character; a heading that takes more than half of a passage on its own is now left out (it made 1,480 passages exceed the cap in an intermediate build; the rendered text was always capped at retrieval time).
- Head-entity look-ups: BM25 alone answers 'Who was Napoleon?' with 'Napoleon Dynamite' and 'What is DNA?' with 'DNA polymerase' (short passages win the length normalisation). The library therefore stores an exact title look-up (hashes of the content terms of every title, 4 numbers per key): when the query's content terms equal a title's, the candidate is that article's lead. Several titles can share a key ('Mercury (planet)', 'Mercury (element)'); the title without a disambiguator (Wikipedia's primary topic) wins, else the more important one, and the gate sees an 'ambiguous' feature.
- Question-answer layer: the time-sensitive filter is deliberately aggressive (precision over coverage); it also removes some stable questions (for example 'Who is the only US President never to have married?' and questions that mention 'now' in a stable sense). Normalised duplicates whose answers disagree are dropped together. TriviaQA rc.nocontext has one row per evidence document (138,384 train rows = 76,523 unique questions); the store keeps each question once.
- Dev labels are strict: an NQ-open train question carries usually one annotated answer span, so a stored paraphrase with another correct span counts as a miss; dev qa precision is therefore a lower bound. The NQ-open validation questions carry up to five annotations.
- Gate operating point: the threshold is the smallest at which every dev set separately (NQ train LOO, TriviaQA train LOO, head-entity probes) reaches precision 0.5. A pooled floor with equal set weights chose p >= 0.06 in an intermediate build: 91% pooled dev coverage at precision 0.52, but dev NQ alone 82% coverage at 27% precision, hidden by the easy probe set.
- The index sweep ran before the primary-topic rule of the title table was added; the differences between its rows come from the index alone. It used dev questions only; the evaluation sets were measured once, on the final library.
- The cross-check feature (the qa answer occurs in the wiki candidate's text) reads the wiki candidate's text block for every query that has a qa candidate; this is included in the read set above.
- Head-entity probes are synthetic ('What is X?', 'Who was X?', 'Tell me about X.', 'X', ...) for the 10,000 most important titles of each half of a hash split of the titles; their label is 'the returned passage belongs to the probed article', which ignores whether a different article would also have been a good answer.
- v1 comparison: the v1 E1 drew its TriviaQA dev and evaluation samples from the 17,944 validation rows, which hold only 9,961 unique questions (one row per evidence document), so its dev and evaluation samples could share questions; v2 samples unique questions and takes its TriviaQA dev questions from the train split.
- The v1 pool's passages reached 510 BPE tokens (non-Latin text under the bytes / 3.3 proxy); every v2 passage is at most 160 tokens (checked over the whole pool).
- Query-type filter: an earlier version treated any first/second-person word as a personal message, which excluded 7% of NQ-open, 5% of TriviaQA and 3% of PopQA questions (song titles such as 'What genre is Thinking of You?', regnal numerals such as 'David I of Scotland', quoted phrases). The final rule ignores quoted text and everything after a title word and counts only the asker or the assistant as subject; search-style phrases of up to twelve words are look-ups, and word problems (two numbers and an arithmetic cue) are math. The gate was refitted after this change.
